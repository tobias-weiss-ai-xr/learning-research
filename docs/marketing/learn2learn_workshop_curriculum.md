## Day 1: Understanding How Learning Works

### Module 1: The Science of Memory (90 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand working memory limitations (4±1 items)
- Learn the forgetting curve and how to fight it
- Apply encoding strategies for better retention

**Content:**

#### Working Memory Limits
**Key Finding:** Working memory holds ~4±1 items at once

**Evidence:** 162 papers on cognitive load

**Practical Application:**
- Chunk information into groups of 4-7
- Avoid multitasking during learning
- Use external aids (notes, diagrams) to offload working memory

**Exercise (15 min):**
```
Task: Memorize this list of 16 items:

apple  book   car    dog
egg    fish   grape  house
ice    juice  kite   lamp
moon   nest   orange piano

Strategy 1: Try to memorize all 16 at once (2 min)
Strategy 2: Chunk into groups of 4 (2 min)
Compare: Which was easier? Why?
```

#### The Forgetting Curve
**Key Finding:** Without review, 70% of information is forgotten within 24 hours

**Evidence:** 1,732 papers on memory/retention

**Practical Application:**
- Review within 24 hours of learning
- Use spaced intervals: 1 day, 3 days, 1 week, 2 weeks, 1 month
- Test yourself, don't just re-read

**Exercise (20 min):**
```
Task: Learn 10 vocabulary words in a new language

Group A: Study for 10 minutes, then stop
Group B: Study for 10 minutes, test immediately, test again in 24 hours

Measure: Recall after 1 day, 1 week, 1 month
Expected: Group B retains 3x more
```

#### Encoding Strategies
**Key Finding:** Multiple encoding pathways improve retention

**Evidence:** Dual-coding theory, 162 cognitive load papers

**Practical Application:**
- Combine visual + verbal (dual-coding)
- Create associations and connections
- Use emotional engagement when possible

**Exercise (20 min):**
```
Task: Learn these 5 concepts:

1. Spaced repetition
2. Active recall
3. Cognitive load
4. Metacognition
5. Transfer

Strategy: Create a visual diagram + story connecting all 5

Share: Present your diagram to a partner
```

**Takeaway:** Memory is not passive storage—it's active construction

### Module 2: Active Learning vs. Passive Review (90 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand the testing effect
- Apply active recall techniques
- Design effective practice sessions

**Content:**

#### The Testing Effect
**Key Finding:** Active recall strengthens memory 2-3x more than passive review

**Evidence:** Embedded in 1,732 memory papers

**Practical Application:**
- Test yourself before you feel ready
- Hide answers and force retrieval
- Embrace the struggle—it's when learning happens

**Exercise (25 min):**
```
Task: Read a 2-page article on learning science

Group A: Re-read the article twice (10 min)
Group B: Read once, then write everything you remember (10 min)

Test: Both groups take a quiz after 1 week
Expected: Group B scores 40% higher
```

#### Question Types That Work
**Best Practices:**
- Retrieval questions: "What are the 3 principles of X?"
- Application questions: "How would you use X in situation Y?"
- Explanation questions: "Explain X to someone who knows nothing"

**Avoid:**
- Recognition questions: "Which of these is X?" (too easy)
- Copy-paste questions: Just copying without processing

**Exercise (20 min):**
```
Task: Convert passive content to active questions

Passive: "Read chapter 3 on project management"
Active: "After reading chapter 3, answer:
  1. What are the 5 phases of project management?
  2. Which phase do you struggle with most?
  3. How would you apply this to your current project?"

Practice: Convert 3 pieces of your own learning content
```

#### Spaced Practice Sessions
**Key Finding:** 3 × 20-minute sessions > 1 × 60-minute session

**Evidence:** Spaced repetition research, 1,732 papers

**Practical Application:**
- Break study into 20-30 minute chunks
- Space sessions over days, not hours
- Use the "forgetting edge"—review just before you forget

**Exercise (15 min):**
```
Task: Design your ideal learning session

Current: "I study for 2 hours on Sunday"
Better: "I study for 25 minutes, 6 times this week"

Create: Your personalized spaced practice schedule
```

**Takeaway:** Struggle is not failure—it's the feeling of learning happening

### Module 3: Managing Cognitive Load (90 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Identify cognitive overload in your learning
- Apply strategies to reduce extraneous load
- Design learning materials for optimal load

**Content:**

#### Types of Cognitive Load
**Intrinsic Load:** Difficulty of the material itself (can't change)
**Extraneous Load:** Poor design, distractions (can reduce)
**Germane Load:** Processing that builds understanding (want to increase)

**Evidence:** 162 papers on cognitive load theory

**Practical Application:**
- Eliminate distractions (phone, notifications, tabs)
- Focus on one task at a time
- Use templates and checklists to reduce mental overhead

**Exercise (20 min):**
```
Task: Audit your current learning environment

Distractions:
- Phone notifications: ___
- Browser tabs open: ___
- Background noise: Yes/No
- Multitasking: Yes/No

Action Plan: Remove 3 distractions this week
```

#### Chunking Strategies
**Key Finding:** Working memory holds 4±1 items

**Practical Application:**
- Break complex topics into 4-7 chunks
- Use hierarchical organization
- Create mental models and frameworks

**Exercise (25 min):**
```
Task: Chunk a complex topic you're learning

Example: Learning programming
Level 1: 5 main concepts (variables, loops, functions, objects, errors)
Level 2: Each concept has 4-7 sub-concepts
Level 3: Each sub-concept has examples

Create: Your chunking diagram for your current learning goal
```

#### Dual-Coding and Multimedia
**Key Finding:** Visual + verbal together > either alone

**Evidence:** Cognitive load and multimedia learning research

**Practical Application:**
- Create diagrams alongside text
- Use animations for processes
- Combine narration with visuals (not text + narration)

**Exercise (15 min):**
```
Task: Convert text to dual-coded format

Original: 500-word explanation of a process
Dual-coded: Diagram + 100-word explanation

Create: Dual-coded version of one concept you're learning
```

**Takeaway:** Good learning design respects working memory limits

## Day 2: Building Your Personal Learning System

### Module 4: Spaced Repetition Systems (90 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Understand how spaced repetition algorithms work
- Choose the right SRS for your needs
- Implement and maintain a personal SRS

**Content:**

#### How SRS Algorithms Work
**Core Principle:** Review at the edge of forgetting

**Common Algorithms:**
- **SM-2:** Original Anki algorithm (simple, effective)
- **FSRS:** Modern algorithm (more accurate)
- **Custom:** Your own rules based on your needs

**Evidence:** 1,732 papers on memory/retention

**Practical Application:**
- Start with established tools (Anki, RemNote)
- Don't over-optimize early—just start
- Review daily, even if only 10 minutes

**Exercise (25 min):**
```
Task: Set up your SRS

Option 1: Anki (free, powerful)
  - Install app
  - Create deck for your current learning
  - Add 10 cards (front/back)
  - Complete first review session

Option 2: Paper-based (simpler)
  - Create 3 boxes: Day 1, Day 3, Day 7
  - Write cards
  - Sort and review daily

Choose: Your SRS method and implement today
```

#### Card Design Best Practices
**Good Cards:**
- Atomic: One fact per card
- Clear: Unambiguous question
- Context: Enough information to answer
- Useful: Actually important to remember

**Bad Cards:**
- Too complex: Multiple concepts on one card
- Vague: "What about X?" (which aspect?)
- Trivial: Unimportant details
- Copy-paste: No processing involved

**Exercise (30 min):**
```
Task: Create 10 high-quality SRS cards

From: Your current learning material
To: 10 atomic, clear, useful cards

Review: Exchange cards with a partner
Feedback: Are they clear? Atomic? Useful?

Iterate: Improve based on feedback
```

#### Maintaining Your SRS
**Key Challenge:** Consistency over months/years

**Strategies:**
- Daily reviews (10-30 minutes)
- Don't let reviews pile up
- Adjust card load if overwhelmed
- Celebrate streaks and milestones

**Exercise (15 min):**
```
Task: Create your SRS maintenance plan

Daily: When and where will you review?
Weekly: How will you add new cards?
Monthly: How will you review progress?
Quarterly: How will you adjust your system?

Commit: Share your plan with an accountability partner
```

**Takeaway:** Consistency beats intensity—10 minutes daily > 2 hours weekly

### Module 5: Adaptive Learning Strategies (90 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Assess your current knowledge accurately
- Adjust difficulty based on performance
- Create personalized learning pathways

**Content:**

#### Self-Assessment Techniques
**Key Finding:** Most people overestimate their knowledge

**Evidence:** 4,495 papers on personalization/adaptive learning

**Practical Application:**
- Use the "Feynman technique": Explain to a 10-year-old
- Test before you feel ready
- Track confidence vs. accuracy

**Exercise (20 min):**
```
Task: Self-assess your knowledge on a topic

Rate your confidence (1-10): ___
Take a test on the topic: Score ___%
Compare: Confidence vs. Accuracy = Calibration gap

Action: How will you improve calibration?
```

#### Difficulty Adjustment
**Target:** 70-80% success rate for optimal learning

**Too Easy (>90%):** Boredom, no growth
**Too Hard (<60%):** Frustration, giving up
**Just Right (70-80%):** Challenge with support

**Evidence:** Zone of Proximal Development research

**Practical Application:**
- If >85% success: Increase difficulty
- If <60% success: Add scaffolding
- Track and adjust regularly

**Exercise (25 min):**
```
Task: Design adaptive difficulty for your learning

Current level: Beginner/Intermediate/Advanced
Success rate: ___%
Adjustment:
  If >85%: What's harder content?
  If <60%: What scaffolding do you need?

Create: Your difficulty adjustment rules
```

#### Learning Pathways
**Key Finding:** One-size-fits-all fails 60%+ of learners

**Practical Application:**
- Map prerequisites for your goal
- Create multiple paths based on background
- Allow skipping for prior knowledge

**Exercise (25 min):**
```
Task: Map your learning pathway

Goal: What do you want to learn?
Prerequisites: What do you need first?
Current state: Where are you now?
Path: What's your route from A to B?
Milestones: How will you know you're progressing?

Create: Visual map of your learning journey
```

**Takeaway:** Learning should feel challenging but achievable

### Module 6: Motivation and Metacognition (90 min)

**Learning Objectives:**
- Apply Self-Determination Theory to your learning
- Build metacognitive awareness
- Create sustainable learning habits

**Content:**

#### Self-Determination Theory (SDT)
**Three Needs:**
1. **Autonomy:** Choice and control over your learning
2. **Competence:** Visible progress and mastery
3. **Relatedness:** Connection to others and purpose

**Evidence:** 848 papers on motivation

**Practical Application:**
- Set your own goals (not imposed)
- Track and celebrate progress
- Connect learning to larger purpose

**Exercise (20 min):**
```
Task: Audit your learning against SDT

Autonomy: Do you choose what/how/when to learn? Yes/No
Competence: Can you see your progress? Yes/No
Relatedness: Does this connect to your purpose? Yes/No

Action: Improve 1 SDT component this week
```

#### Metacognitive Awareness
**Key Finding:** Learners who monitor understanding perform better

**Practical Application:**
- Ask: "Do I really understand this?"
- Track: What do I know vs. don't know?
- Reflect: What worked? What didn't?

**Exercise (25 min):**
```
Task: Build metacognitive habits

Before learning:
  - What do I already know?
  - What do I want to learn?
  - How will I know I've learned it?

During learning:
  - Am I understanding? (1-5 scale)
  - Do I need to slow down?
  - What questions do I have?

After learning:
  - What did I learn? (summarize)
  - What's still unclear?
  - What will I review?

Create: Your metacognitive checklist
```

#### Building Learning Habits
**Key Finding:** Habits > motivation for long-term success

**Strategies:**
- Habit stacking: "After X, I will learn for 15 minutes"
- Environment design: Make learning easy, distraction hard
- Identity: "I am a learner" vs. "I'm trying to learn"

**Evidence:** Habit formation research

**Practical Application:**
- Start small (5-10 minutes daily)
- Stack onto existing habits
- Track streaks and celebrate

**Exercise (25 min):**
```
Task: Design your learning habit

Trigger: What existing habit will you stack onto?
  Example: "After morning coffee, I will review SRS cards"
Action: What exactly will you do?
  Example: "Open Anki, complete daily reviews (10 min)"
Reward: How will you celebrate?
  Example: "Check off on calendar, enjoy coffee"

Create: Your habit formula and start today
```

**Takeaway:** Systems > goals—build habits that make learning automatic
